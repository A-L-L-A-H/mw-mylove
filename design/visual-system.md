# A-L-L-A-H.COM visual system

## Design read

Reading this as a multi-destination knowledge and creative universe for curious readers, in a punk editorial zine language, dial ENERGY 3 / RHYTHM 3 / MOTION 1.

## Shared choices

- Ink black and dirty white carry most surfaces so dense editorial type stays readable.
- Signal yellow marks the primary way forward; magenta and electric blue mark selected worlds and story threads.
- Brush lettering belongs to the identity and short headlines. A clear display face handles labels and readable body copy.
- Torn edges, stamped numbers and photocopy halftone repeat as the shared motif. Each destination changes its composition and story objects.
- Motion stays to visible hover and focus feedback; the pages do not rely on parallax or long entrance effects.
- Cosmic imagery uses moons, light, geometry and architecture. It does not portray Allah as a person or fabricate sacred text.

## Page-specific composition cues

- Home: moonlit city, large identity, world preview and entry path.
- Nine Worlds: illustrated atlas with portal links and a visible relationship path.
- MoonWitness: documentary fragments, story collections and archive timeline.
- Arsy: signal tower, transmission paths and system diagrams.
- Human-Pen: open notebook, writing desk and manuscript fragments.
- Sidra: connected knowledge paths and concept map.
- RockSoul DX: experimental workshop with playable project fragments.
- Crayon: hand-drawn studio wall and visual collections.
- Sin Can: clipped archive and annotated investigation board.
- Mythos: celestial manuscript and narrative map, with no invented canon.
- Angel With Shotgun: law and society editorial using civic symbols and documents.
- Explore: cross-universe search and browse paths, separate from Sidra's knowledge map.
- Docs: reading-focused technical archive with diagrams and reference index.
- Community: repository-led collaboration view; no fabricated people or testimonials.
- About: manifesto and ecosystem map, with source-backed principles only.
